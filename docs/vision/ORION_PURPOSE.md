# What ORION is for (Nick, 2026-09-29)

This is the test every feature is measured against. Written by Nick with ChatGPT; kept verbatim in substance.

ORION is Nick's **external executive function**: a second version of him that holds his goals, priorities, ambitions, routines and responsibilities, but is not affected by tiredness, procrastination, executive dysfunction, or whatever excuse feels compelling in the moment.

It does not just answer questions or run computer commands. It actively manages his life *with* him: understands what he intended, observes what actually happened, notices when he is falling behind or off-plan, intervenes, and reorganizes the plan around reality. Planned sleep at midnight but awake at 3 AM → recognize it, tell him to go to bed, and adjust tomorrow around the consequences (protect class, schedule a nap, move work, check back).

**Close the loop.** A dismissible "work on your assignment" is not enough. If he hasn't started, ORION notices. "I'm starving, let me eat first" is reasonable → give 20–30 minutes, then come back: "Okay, you ate. Now we're starting." Ignored → escalate rather than vanish: stronger notifications, lock-screen presence, repeated check-ins, and — where technically possible and explicitly permitted — temporarily restricting distracting apps.

**Not a rigid scheduler.** Reality overrides the plan: a friend's invitation, class running late, exhaustion, a task taking twice as long, a worthwhile spontaneous choice. ORION adapts instead of scolding. Its standing question: *given Nick's goals, commitments, current state and what has actually happened today, what should he do next?*

**Personalization and conversation quality matter.** ORION must know him well enough to tell a legitimate reason to change the plan from rationalized procrastination, and talk like someone who understands him — not canned motivational language. Sometimes flexibility; sometimes "No. You've been avoiding this for an hour. Get up. We're doing it now."

**The point is structure, not doing everything for him.** He works hard once engaged; initiation, transitions, prioritization and maintaining routines are where he falls apart. ORION bridges that: **plan → remind → observe → intervene → follow up → adapt → repeat.**

Computer control, integrations, the phone app, memory, scheduling and notifications all serve that mission; they are not the mission. If ORION can open Opera but can't reliably get him out of bed, get him studying when he said he would, stop doomscrolling, recover a day that went sideways, and follow through on what he cares about, it is a cool AI app — not ORION.
